## The Value Equation

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

- **Dream Outcome** — the gap between the buyer's current state and the state they want. Status-elevation framing beats functional framing: emphasize how the result makes them look to the people they answer to.
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** — their belief they will actually get it. Built with proof: track record, case studies, guarantees, specifics.
- **Time Delay** — how long until they see anything. Deliver an emotional win early; the full result can come later.
- **Effort & Sacrifice** — everything it costs them beyond money: hours, meetings, internal selling, risk.

Two traps Hormozi names: most sellers lazily inflate the top of the equation with bigger claims. The harder, more defensible work is shrinking the bottom — less time, less effort. Done-for-you beats do-it-yourself at every price point for this reason.

**Sponsorship translation.** The sponsor's dream outcome is never "logo on a banner." It is hires made, leads met, deals influenced, brand associated with something their buyers care about. Likelihood is proven with audience data, past sponsor results, and a fulfillment report. Time delay shrinks when the sponsor gets value before the event (intro emails, content, a talent pipeline) instead of only at it. Effort shrinks when the org does the work: the sponsor writes one check and shows up.

## Grand Slam Offer

"An offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available, combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee with a money model that allows you to get paid to get new customers."

Category-of-one positioning: when the offer can't be compared, the buyer stops price-shopping and decides on value. Priority order Hormozi gives: **starving crowd (market) > offer strength > persuasion skill**. Picking sponsors who already need this audience matters more than the copy.

## The 5-step offer creation process

1. **Identify the dream outcome.** The end state the buyer craves, not the mechanism. ("Hire 5 great engineering interns by May", not "booth at our career fair".)
2. **List every problem** in the way, sorted by the four value levers: won't be worth it (outcome), won't work for me (likelihood), too hard (effort), too slow (time).
3. **Turn each problem into a solution statement.** "How to [get outcome] without [problem]."
4. **Generate delivery vehicles** for each solution, using the Delivery Cube:
   - Attention level: 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many
   - Effort type: DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you
   - Medium: in person / phone / email / video / chat
   - Format: audio / video / written
   - Speed: 24/7 / business hours / within N hours
   - The 10x / 1/10th test: what would you deliver if they paid 10× the price? If they paid 1/10th and you still had to make them succeed, what would you keep?
5. **Trim and stack.** Cut high-cost low-value items first, then low-cost low-value. Keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value. Bundle what remains into named components, each with a stated value; the stack's total stated value should be a large multiple of the price. Hormozi's worked example: seven named components totaling $4,351 in stated value, priced at $599.

The Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum: pick the point where the thing sells well AND can actually be delivered. Never promise what fulfillment can't cash — in sponsorship this is fatal, because renewal is the real revenue.

## Offer enhancers

**Scarcity** (function of quantity) — three types: limited slots, limited bonuses, never again. Service versions: total cap ("we take 6 sponsors"), growth cap, cohort cap. Rule: only claim caps that are literally true, then communicate them as social proof ("4 of 6 spots taken").

**Urgency** (function of time) — four methods: rolling cohorts, seasonal deadlines, promotional/pricing deadlines, exploding opportunity. An event date is real urgency; sponsorship inherits a true deadline for free.

**Bonuses** — a stack of named components beats one blob. The 11 rules, compressed: always have them; name each for its benefit; tie each to a specific objection or obstacle; explain what it is and why it exists; prove it works; price each one and justify the price; prefer tools and checklists over more content; solve the buyer's *next* problem preemptively; never discount the core offer — add bonuses instead.

**Guarantees** — "the primary conversion lever" because they attack perceived likelihood. Frame: "If you do not get X in Y time, we will Z." Four types:
1. *Unconditional* — money back, no questions. Strongest, riskiest.
2. *Conditional* — tied to defined conditions; best default. Strong sponsorship-shaped versions: make-good delivery (undelivered asset gets replaced with one of equal or greater value), extended service (we keep promoting you after the event until impressions hit the number), credit toward next year.
3. *Anti-guarantee* — "all sales final", justified; filters for serious buyers of custom work.
4. *Implied / performance* — pay-per-outcome, revenue share. Maps to per-lead or per-hire sponsorship pricing.
Stack them (e.g. unconditional 30-day + conditional performance), and name the guarantee.

## Naming: the MAGIC formula

Use 3–5 of: **M**agnetic reason why, **A**vatar called out, **G**oal stated, **I**nterval, **C**ontainer word. "The Spring Engineering Talent Pipeline Partnership" beats "Gold Tier". Renaming is also the cheapest way to refresh a stale offer.

## Pricing posture

The niche-down ladder: the same product priced to a narrower, better-fit avatar commands multiples ($19 generic → $1,997 hyper-specific in Hormozi's example). Price communicates positioning; premium price with a stacked offer and guarantee outperforms discounting. Never compete on price — when a sponsor pushes back, trade scope, not price (see the Enns reference for the mechanics).

**Honest caveat** (from The Power Moves' critical commentary): "charge premium" is not a universal law — it presumes differentiated value and a buyer who cares. For a first-time property with no track record, the likelihood lever is weak; compensate with a strong guarantee and a smaller, real ask rather than fantasy pricing.
