# Sinek: Start With Why (The Golden Circle)

Distilled from Simon Sinek's "Start With Why — How Great Leaders Inspire Action" talk. The complete transcript ships with this skill at `references/start-with-why-sinek-transcript.txt` and was read in full at authoring time; read it directly when exact wording or the full arc of an example is needed.

## The Golden Circle

Three rings, inside out: **Why → How → What.** Everyone knows *what* they do; some know *how* (the differentiated process); very few can state *why* — the purpose, cause, or belief, where "why" is never "to make a profit" (that's a result). Inspired organizations think, act, and communicate from the inside out; everyone else goes outside in, "from the clearest thing to the fuzziest thing."

The Apple demonstration: outside-in is "We make great computers. They're beautifully designed. Wanna buy one?" — features, then a shrug. Inside-out is "Everything we do, we believe in challenging the status quo... The way we challenge the status quo is by making our products beautifully designed... We just happen to make great computers. Wanna buy one?" Same facts, reversed order, entirely different pull.

**"People don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it. And what you do simply proves what you believe."**

Sinek grounds it in brain structure: the neocortex processes language and rational argument (the *what* level); the limbic brain drives feelings, trust, and all decision-making, and has no capacity for language. Features inform; belief moves. This is why a decision that fails on facts still "doesn't feel right."

Supporting arcs in the transcript: the Wright brothers (belief-driven, out-resourced, won) vs. Samuel Pierpont Langley (funded, credentialed, chasing the result — quit the day they flew); TiVo (best product, communicated only *what*, commercial failure); Dr. King ("I have a dream," not "I have a plan" — 250,000 people showed up for what *they* believed, not for him). The law of diffusion: innovators and early adopters buy on belief; the majority waits for someone else to go first, so the believers are the beachhead.

**"The goal is not to do business with everybody who needs what you have. The goal is to do business with people who believe what you believe."**

## Sponsorship translation

1. **The org's why opens the story.** A sponsorship packet that starts with "we are a student org founded in..." is outside-in. Start with the belief ("we believe the people building things here deserve backers who show up early"), then the how, then the event as proof. The *what* — the event, the audience numbers — serves as evidence of the belief, which is exactly the order Sinek prescribes.
2. **Belief-fit is a sponsor qualification criterion.** The best sponsors are the ones who believe what the org believes — they renew, they activate hard, they tell colleagues. A sponsor buying only reach is a transaction; a sponsor buying the why is a partner. Ask in discovery what the company believes about this audience; the answer predicts the relationship.
3. **Why-first cold emails.** One line of belief can carry a cold email's hook where a feature list dies. It also self-selects: recipients who share the belief reply, which is the reply you want.
4. **Limits, honestly held.** Why-language without proof is a poster. The golden circle sets the *order* of the message; the value equation and audience data still have to cash the check. Belief opens the door, evidence closes the deal.
