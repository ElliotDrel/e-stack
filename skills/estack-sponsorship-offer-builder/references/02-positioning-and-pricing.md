## Why this matters for sponsorship

A sponsorship ask is a professional-services sale, not a product sale. The org is selling access and expertise about its own audience. Enns' whole system is built for exactly this: position as the expert on the thing the buyer can't get elsewhere, diagnose before prescribing, and price the client rather than the package.

## Positioning: the expert, not the supplicant

The Manifesto's first proclamation: **we will specialize.** Specialization is what creates negotiating power — fewer substitutes, deeper pattern recognition, premium pricing, simpler messaging. For a sponsorship property this reads: don't sell "sponsorship of our event", sell "the only direct channel to [this specific audience] in [this context]". If the org can't state what it is the *only* one of, that positioning sentence is the first thing to fix.

Posture follows: an expert diagnoses before prescribing and does not do free pitching. Translated: no proposal before a discovery conversation (the sponsorship-market reference lands the same rule from the sponsorship side). The proposal confirms a conversation that already happened; it never opens one.

## The value conversation (run it before any number is quoted)

Enns' flow, in order:

1. **Desired future state.** Get the buyer to describe, enthusiastically and in their words, what better looks like. Not features, outcomes.
2. **Metrics.** How will they know it worked? Which numbers move?
3. **Value quantification.** What is that worth — revenue gained, cost avoided, and the emotional/strategic value (often the biggest component)?
4. **Price guidance.** Say a range out loud before writing anything, anchored high ("engagements like this run $X to $Y"), and watch the reaction.
5. **Options, then solutions.** Only now design what to sell them.

"Value is like beauty — it's in the eye of the beholder." The same asset list is worth different amounts to different sponsors, which is why fixed tier menus systematically leave money on the table.

## The six rules of Pricing Creativity

1. **Price the client, not the job.** Same deliverable, different value per buyer.
2. **Offer options.** One option asks "is this worth $50,000?" — a question brains answer badly, usually by seeking other bids. Three options ask "which of these is the best value?" — comparison happens inside your proposal instead of against competitors.
3. **Anchor high.** Present the highest option first. It is "not there to be sold"; it exists to make the middle option feel reasonable. (Nobel-vintage anchoring research; first numbers dominate.)
4. **Say a price before showing a proposal.** A spoken range costs nothing and surfaces objections while they're still cheap.
5. **Master the value conversation.** The longest chapter in the book; the flow above.
6. **Limit unpaid proposals to one page.** One page forces clarity and keeps the selling in the conversation where it belongs.

Case data from the 2Bobs episode: a firm at $2M revenue / 4% margin moved to $3.5M / 45% margin inside a year largely on narrowed focus plus one-page, three-option proposals; another client raised a single proposal from $65k to $167k with one book principle.

## The three-option structure for a sponsorship ask

- **Option 3 (anchor, presented first):** everything — category exclusivity, year-round presence, the full done-for-you activation. Priced where it would be genuinely worth doing. Most sponsors won't take it; its job is context, and occasionally someone says yes.
- **Option 2 (the target):** the offer actually designed for this sponsor from discovery. Priced at the real ask.
- **Option 1 (the floor):** a smaller, still-real package. Never a throwaway; it's the credible fallback that keeps a "no budget this year" sponsor in the relationship.

Options differ by **scope and access, not by enthusiasm**. When a sponsor negotiates, move them between options or trade scope; never discount an option in place — a price that drops under pressure teaches the buyer the first number was fiction.

Clients also pay for **price certainty**: a fixed number, guaranteed, is itself a feature worth naming.
