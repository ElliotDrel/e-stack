## Cialdini: the 7 principles, tuned for a follow-up sequence

1. **Reciprocity.** People give back after receiving. Works best early, when the relationship is thin — exactly where a cold sequence lives. The ethics line: give something of real value with no hidden agenda ("manipulative reciprocity is when the gift has a hidden agenda the recipient would reject if they knew about it"). For sponsorship: audience insights, a relevant intro, a market data point — given, not teased.
2. **Commitment & consistency.** Small yeses precede big ones; attitudes follow behavior. Ask for a 15-minute call, not a contract.
3. **Social proof.** Under uncertainty, people copy comparable others. Specific and small beats grand: "two firms in your space took spots last month" outperforms "everyone loves us".
4. **Authority.** Expertise lowers resistance: knowing their business, citing your audience data precisely, writing like someone who has done this before.
5. **Liking.** Similarity and genuine warmth signal "this person understands my interests". Personalization that proves you actually looked is the honest form.
6. **Scarcity.** The most powerful urgency lever "and the most dangerous when misused". Deploy only after value is established, and only when literally true. Premature or fake scarcity signals desperation and kills credibility.
7. **Unity.** Shared identity ("as a [city] company", "as fellow alumni") outperforms behavior appeals — when the identity genuinely exists.

**Mechanism vs structure (Semalytics):** the principles are the emotional mechanism inside a message, not its outline. Pick the one or two that fit this buyer — proof-driven buyers respond to authority; risk-averse buyers to loss framing and certainty; relationship buyers need warmth before logic. One mechanism per message; stacking all seven into one email reads as a con.

## Blount: why sequences work

- **Universal Law of Need:** desperation repels — "the more you communicate that you need the sale, the more likely they say no." Never let a follow-up smell like need; the graceful exit is what keeps the door open for next season.
- **30-Day Rule:** prospecting done in any 30-day window pays out over the following 90. Sponsorship outreach starts months before the event.
- **Law of Replacement:** prospects fall out constantly; add new ones rather than squeezing dead ones harder.
- **The three Ps that stall people:** procrastination, perfectionism, paralysis by analysis. The cure is shipping the sequence on schedule, not polishing message four.
- **The triple touch:** alternate channels — email, phone, LinkedIn — instead of stacking one. Familiarity compounds: the fourth touch lands differently when the name is already known.
- **Message rules:** prospects take meetings for their reasons, not yours. Every touch is "quick, simple, direct, and relevant", answers "what's in it for me", and makes one low-risk ask. The "because" bridge — one specific, relevant reason this message is for them — separates outreach from spam.
- **RBOs:** "Not interested" in two seconds is a reflex, not a decision; "send me some info" is usually a test — respond with a narrowing question. A real objection (price, timing, exclusivity) is engagement: acknowledge, answer, re-ask. Turnaround: anchor (prepared response) → disrupt (agree, take away the fight: "I figured you'd be slammed") → ask again.
