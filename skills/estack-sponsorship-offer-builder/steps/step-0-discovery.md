# Phase 0: Discovery — Assets, Sponsor Profile, and the Packet Critique

Read `references/03-sponsorship-market.md` before running this phase, and `references/01-hormozi-value-equation.md` if not already loaded. This phase produces the raw material the offer is built from. Run it as a conversation, a few questions per turn, not as a form to fill out.

## What this phase must produce

1. An **asset inventory** — everything the org can actually deliver, honestly rated.
2. A **sponsor profile** — who is being sold to, specific enough to name real companies.
3. If an existing packet/proposal/deck was provided: a **critique** against the Value Equation and the known packet mistakes.

Capture all three in the conversation as you go; they become the first sections of the offer file in Phase 1.

## 0a. The org's why (one question, early)

Ask why the org exists, in one sentence that isn't "to run the event". Push past results ("grow", "fund the club") to belief — see `references/07-start-with-why.md`. If the user can't state it yet, note that and move on; a candidate why usually surfaces during the asset conversation ("what do people say the org did for them?").

## 0b. Asset inventory

Walk the categories below and ask what the org actually has. For each claimed asset, ask the two qualifying questions: *is it real* (deliverable this season, with proof it exists) and *what sponsor outcome does it serve* (an asset that serves none gets cut — "no assets, no sales", but also no fictional assets).

Categories to probe, from the practitioner research:
- **Audience access** — events, demo days, workshops, newsletters, community channels. Get real numbers: how many people, what demographics, what engagement. Unverified numbers get marked `[assumption — verify]`.
- **Recruiting pipeline** — resume books, intro dinners, first-look interviews, internship funnels. Usually the single most valuable asset class for B2B sponsors of talent-dense communities, and usually underpriced.
- **Content & reach** — social accounts, YouTube/podcast, blog, email list. Real engagement numbers only.
- **Brand association** — naming rights (event, stage, workshop series, prize), "official X of Y" status, category exclusivity. Exclusivity is an asset even when nothing else changes.
- **Product access** — sampling, product placement, users for beta tests, feedback sessions.
- **People's time** — founder/leader intros, advisory sessions, speaking slots for the sponsor's people.
- **Proof & data** — post-event reports, testimonials, photos. The *fulfillment report itself* is a sellable asset (see the fulfillment section of the market reference).

Then the inventory-building questions from the research: what's missing that sponsors would want? What do comparable properties offer? What do they *not* offer that this org could? Five minutes on gaps often surfaces the differentiating asset.

## 0c. Sponsor profile

Ask who the sponsor is, concretely. If the answer is "any company with money", coach it narrower using the audience-overlap rule: sponsors buy audiences, so the profile falls out of who wants *this* audience.

- What kind of company gets direct value from this audience — as talent, as customers, as users?
- Local businesses, national brands, VCs, alumni-run companies, tools vendors? Different profiles want different assets and read different tones; pick a primary.
- Who sponsors comparable properties? Who competes with *those* sponsors? ("If one bank has interest, I bet they all do.")
- Belief-fit: which of these companies would plausibly share the org's why? Those go to the top of the list.
- Who is the buyer inside the company — Brand, Marketing, BD, University Relations, CSR? What does *that person* get judged on? (Their metric is the dream outcome the offer must serve.)

Name 3–5 real example companies before leaving this step. Abstract profiles produce abstract offers.

## 0d. Critique of the existing packet (only if one exists)

Read the provided packet and score every page against two lenses:

1. **The Value Equation** — for each page: does it raise the sponsor's dream outcome, raise likelihood (proof), cut time, or cut effort? Pages that do none (org history, mission essays, board lists, generic "opportunities" grids) get flagged for cut or rewrite.
2. **The known mistakes** (market reference) — gold/silver/bronze tiers, org-as-hero framing, no audience data before the ask, no measurable outcomes, tear-off payment form, unprofessional details.

Deliver the critique as a short per-page table (page → what it does now → which lever it fails → fix), then one paragraph on the biggest single gap. Teach while critiquing: name the principle each failure violates so the user learns the lens, not just the fixes.

## Exit check

Confirm with the user before moving to Phase 1: the why (or a marked placeholder), the inventory with real/unverified flags, the named sponsor profile with example companies, and (if applicable) the critique delivered. Then go to `steps/step-1-offer.md`.
