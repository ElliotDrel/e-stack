# Path B: The Cold Email Chain (1 Opener + 3 Follow-ups)

Read `references/04-outreach-psychology.md` before drafting. If `estack-cold-message-writer` and `estack-email-writer` are installed (they ship in the same pack), load both — they own first-touch psychology and email craft respectively; this path owns what's sponsorship-specific. The finalized offer from Phase 1 is the input.

## What this path produces

`sponsor-email-chain.md`: four complete, ready-to-send emails (opener + three follow-ups), each with subject line, body, send-timing note, and a one-line note naming the psychological mechanism it runs — so the user can adapt the sequence without breaking it.

## Before drafting

- **Confirm the target.** The chain is written to the named buyer role from the offer (Brand/Marketing/BD/University Relations), ideally to a real first company from the Phase 0 example list. A chain written to "companies" fails; one written to a person generalizes cleanly.
- **Get a real hook** for the opener: something true about this company (they hire this audience, they sponsor a comparable property, a post their team wrote, a shared belief). Don't fabricate; if the user has none, help them find one or say plainly the opener will be weaker.
- **The offer travels in pieces.** The chain's job is to open a conversation, not to sell the package. No attached packet (the research is unambiguous: cold attachments read as spam). Each email carries at most one offer element as a tease, and the ask is always small: a short call, or "want the audience numbers?"

## The sequence design

Each email: one hook, one mechanism, one ask, short. Vary the angle across the four; never "just bumping this". Default mechanics (adapt to the user's situation):

1. **Opener — the belief + the fit.** Why-first where it works: one line of belief or one line about *them* (their hiring, their market, their post), one line of what the org is with a concrete proof number, one weightless ask. Mechanism: liking/unity via genuine relevance.
2. **Follow-up 1 (+3–4 days) — give something.** Reciprocity, honestly: a real audience insight, a data point, a relevant intro offer. No ask beyond "useful?" or the same small call ask restated once.
3. **Follow-up 2 (+4–5 days) — proof.** Social proof or authority: comparable sponsor outcome, a specific result, the strongest number from the offer's proof section. If a true cap or deadline exists (event date, exclusivity), it can appear here as fact, not pressure.
4. **Follow-up 3 (+5–7 days) — the graceful close.** A clean no-oriented ending ("if this isn't a fit this season, no problem — should I close the file?"). Easy no, relationship preserved for next season. Mechanism: Blount's no-neediness law; the calm exit is what keeps the door open.

Cadence notes from the research: days apart, not hours; consider alternating channels (the triple touch — connect on LinkedIn between emails 2 and 3); most replies come from follow-ups, so the user should commit to sending all four before judging the sequence.

## Truth rules (non-negotiable)

Every number, name, and claim in every email is either verified by the user or does not ship — a cold email is a promise the fulfillment report has to keep. Scarcity/urgency only when literally true. No fake personalization ("loved your post" about a post nobody read).

## Handling replies

- "Send me some info" → it's usually a test: reply with a narrowing question ("happy to — is this more a recruiting conversation or a brand one for you?") plus one tight paragraph, not the packet.
- Any meeting interest → book fast, then offer **Path C** (the meeting script) if it isn't built yet.
- Objections (budget, timing) → real engagement: acknowledge, answer with the relevant offer element, re-ask small. Load `estack-chris-voss` if installed.

## Done when

Four emails exist with subjects, timing, and mechanism notes; each has exactly one ask; the opener's hook is real; nothing contradicts the offer file; total reading burden per email is seconds, not minutes (under ~90 words each is the craft-skill ceiling); and the user has confirmed the sequence against their brand rules.
