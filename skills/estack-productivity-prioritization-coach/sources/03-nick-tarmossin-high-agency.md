## What this source contributes to the skill

The **execution and momentum** half of the skill. RPM (source 01) and the leverage filters (source 02) decide *what* to do. This source covers *how to keep doing it* — turning a tightened plan into sustained motion through decomposition, gamified wins, and an iteration loop. It also adds an ambition gut-check at the goal-setting stage.

## Synthesized takeaways

**Ambition gut-check.** Name what you want, then ask one question: does it defy the laws of physics? If not, it is achievable — proceed. If it does, the goal is miscalibrated and needs reshaping. The framing is a permission-granting reality check: almost nothing real is actually impossible, so the bottleneck is decomposition, not feasibility.

**Decompose top-down with first principles.** Break big, hard tasks into small, easy ones by reducing the problem to its fundamental truths and rebuilding from there. The purpose of decomposition is psychological as much as logical: it lets you focus on one small thing at a time so the big picture stops being overwhelming, and it makes each piece small enough to actually finish.

**Gamify to manufacture momentum.** Small, frequently-completed steps act as rewards. Each completion produces a small, frequent dopamine release; frequent reward makes the process self-reinforcing — "addicting" in the productive sense. Once that kicks in you build momentum and start an *upward spiral*: visible results sustain motivation, and not wanting to lose or slow those results sustains discipline. Motivation and discipline here are outputs of seeing results, not prerequisites you must summon first. The lever is making the wins small and frequent enough to start the spiral.

**Treat failure as data collection.** Pursuing the small tasks, you fail often — and that is necessary. You need enough data points to learn what works and what doesn't. Once you have enough: drop what doesn't work, double down on what does, and repeat. Over iterations this builds a curated knowledge base of what actually works for you.

**Outsource the curve when time-constrained.** The failure-and-iteration loop is slow. If you are under a time constraint, shortcut it by learning from people who already ran the loop — up to and including buying their curated knowledge directly — instead of rediscovering it yourself.

**The whole map.** Break big things down into their fundamental truths, then climb back up the ladder to accomplish the thing you want. Nothing is too big; it is only insufficiently decomposed.
