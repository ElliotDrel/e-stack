## What this source contributes to the skill

The **constraint lens** on prioritization: the object worth attacking is not the most important-feeling item, it is the one thing currently limiting the system's output. Everything else is load applied to a non-bottleneck, which does not speed the system up regardless of how hard it is worked.

## Synthesized takeaways

The post's argument, in its own words:

> "One idea from Eliyahu Goldratt's *The Goal* that stuck with me is that constantly working does not necessarily mean you're being productive. In fact, it can be incredibly inefficient."

The illustration it uses: one developer has to approve every software change. That developer works nonstop while five others wait. Everyone else can find work to stay busy, but the system does not move faster. "Instead it's loading unnecessary work onto the bottleneck."

The conclusion:

> "Productivity isn't about maximizing how much time you spend working. It's about identifying what is actually limiting progress and focusing your effort there."

And the question it closes on, which is the usable coaching prompt:

> "How much of what keeps you busy is actually moving you toward your goal?"

## Why it belongs next to source 04

Together, these two sources produce the loop the skill now teaches:

- **Goldratt / this post** answers *what to work on*: the constraint, not the busiest or loudest thing.
- **Hormozi (source 04)** answers *why you stop working on it before it pays*: focus, patience, and frustration tolerance are what carry a constraint through the boring middle.

Stated as one rule: **repeat what compounds, change what constrains.** The compounding behaviors are stable across long horizons. The constraint is the thing that is allowed to change, and it changes on a review, not on a feeling.

## What this source does NOT establish

- It is one person's paraphrase of one idea from a novel. No page reference, no direct quotation of Goldratt.
- It does not address how to *identify* a constraint, which is the hard part in a personal (rather than manufacturing) system where there is no queue length to measure.
- The manufacturing analogy transfers imperfectly. A factory bottleneck is observable from work-in-progress piling up in front of it; a personal bottleneck has no equivalent instrument, which is exactly why people guess and call the guess a bottleneck.
