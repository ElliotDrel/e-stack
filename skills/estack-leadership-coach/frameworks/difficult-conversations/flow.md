# Difficult conversations flow (Omission 3)

<primary_outcome>
The user leaves with a written **Conversation Script**: the behavior named in concrete, observable terms; an opening built on clarity, charity, and resolve; the "joyful accountability" close that puts the choice on the other person; and a plan for the hard moment when they react. If the user has been avoiding the conversation, the selfishness reframe gets delivered before the script is built.
</primary_outcome>

This flow runs when the user needs to confront someone's behavior — an attitude, a mannerism, a political pattern — and has been dreading or dodging it. Grounded in `references/lencioni_the-motive.md` (Omission 3) and `references/van-edwards_cues.md` (warmth/competence in delivery).

---

## When to run this flow

- "I need to talk to someone about their behavior and I keep putting it off"
- "How do I give hard feedback without it blowing up?"
- A person's attitude, mannerism, or politics is hurting the team
- The user is venting about a report instead of talking to them
- The motive gate flagged an avoided conversation

---

## The frame (grounded in *The Motive*)

Lencioni: *"One of the main responsibilities of a leader is to confront difficult, awkward issues quickly and with clarity, charity, and resolve."* The issues are behavioral — *"everything from a team member's annoying mannerisms to poisonous interpersonal dynamics and politics."* Almost every leader balks at these. The cost of balking: *"Failing to confront people quickly about small issues is a guarantee that they will become big issues."*

Two ideas do the heavy lifting:

- **The selfishness reframe.** The reason we avoid these conversations is that they're *"embarrassing and awkward"* — for us. Lencioni's realization: *"holding back and avoiding those conversations was actually an act of selfishness. I wasn't avoiding those conversations for the sake of my employees' feelings, but for my own!"* Avoidance trades your momentary discomfort for their much larger future pain (a bad review, a firing, an exit interview). Naming this is often the unlock for an avoider.

- **Joyful accountability** (Alan Mulally's model). Approach the person *cheerfully but sternly*. Let them know it's *completely up to them* whether they change. Make the consequence clear and kind: if they can't change, you'll still be their friend and think well of them — but they can't keep working here this way. Not passive-aggressive, not condescending. Meant sincerely.

Delivery matters: Van Edwards' warmth and competence cues determine whether the person hears care or attack. High warmth + high resolve is the target — warm enough that they feel respected, resolute enough that they know it's real.

## The flow (run the moves in order)

### Move 1 — Name the behavior in observable terms (Listen)

> **Question:** What's the specific behavior — the thing you'd point to on video, not the label? ("Interrupts people in every meeting," not "is disrespectful.")

Push relentlessly from adjective to observable. "Bad attitude" is not confrontable; "rolls their eyes and goes silent when overruled" is. The whole script depends on this being concrete.

### Move 2 — Check for avoidance and deliver the reframe (Educate)

> **Question:** How long has this been going on, and what's kept you from raising it?

If they've been avoiding it, deliver the selfishness reframe directly: the avoidance is protecting your comfort, not their feelings, and it's setting them up for a worse reckoning later. Charity means telling them now, while it's still small.

**The self-check (ask these verbatim — a "yes" is the tell).** Lencioni closes this omission with two reflection questions. Ask them straight, and hold the user to the answer:

> **Ask yourself:**
> - *"Would you rather learn to live with a person's difficult behaviors than endure an awkward, potentially emotional discussion with them?"*
> - *"Do you find yourself venting about your direct reports' behavioral issues rather than talking with them directly?"*

A "yes" to either is, in Lencioni's words, *"an indication that your leadership motive needs to be adjusted."* If the user is venting to you right now instead of planning the conversation, that *is* the second question answering itself — name it, and turn the venting into the script.

### Move 3 — Build the script with clarity, charity, resolve (Apply + Execute)

Draft the actual language across three beats:

- **Clarity** — name the behavior and its impact, specifically and without hedging. ("In the last three meetings, when a decision didn't go your way, you went quiet and then re-litigated it afterward with others. Here's the effect that has…")
- **Charity** — affirm the person genuinely. This is real, not a compliment sandwich. ("You're sharp and the team respects your thinking, which is exactly why this matters.")
- **Resolve + joyful accountability** — put the choice on them, warmly and firmly. ("It's up to you whether you want to change this. If you do, I'm here to help. If you decide you can't, I'll still respect you — but we can't keep working this way.")

### Move 4 — Plan the hard moment (Execute)

Anticipate the reaction — defensiveness, tears, counter-attack, "who said that?" — and script the user's hold. The goal is to stay warm and resolute without collapsing (backing down) or escalating (getting cold). One or two prepared holds. Capture into the script.

## Artifact template — Conversation Script

<template>

```markdown
# Conversation Script: <person> — <behavior in 3 words>

**The behavior (observable):** <what you'd see on video>
**Impact:** <what it costs the team/org>
**Why now:** <clarity/charity — telling them while it's small is the kind move>

## The opening (clarity)
> <the specific words naming the behavior and its impact>

## The affirmation (charity)
> <genuine, specific — why this person is worth the conversation>

## The choice (resolve + joyful accountability)
> <it's up to you; here's my support if you change; here's the line if you can't>

## When they react
**If they get defensive / deflect / push back:** <the prepared hold — warm and resolute>

## What a good outcome looks like
<observable — behavior change you'd see, or a clean mutual decision>
```

</template>

## Pre-empted shortcuts

- **Don't let the behavior stay an adjective.** "Disrespectful," "not a team player," "negative" are unconfrontable. Force it to observable behavior before scripting anything.
- **Don't build a compliment sandwich.** Charity is genuine respect stated plainly, not padding that buries the message. The person should leave clear on what has to change.
- **Don't skip the choice.** Joyful accountability means the person owns the decision to change. A script that only instructs, without putting the choice and the consequence on them, isn't the model.
- **Don't coach the user out of having it.** If they're looking for permission to keep avoiding, the selfishness reframe is the answer, not "maybe give it more time."
