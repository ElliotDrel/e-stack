# Managing subordinates flow (Omission 2)

<primary_outcome>
The user leaves with a written **Management Cadence**: for the report(s) they've been under-managing, a concrete structure for the three things management actually is (setting direction, staying informed on progress, coaching behavior) plus the one-level-down check that their own managers are doing the same for their people. If the user is hiding behind "I trust them" or "I don't want to micromanage," that gets reframed before the cadence is built.
</primary_outcome>

This flow runs when the user doesn't really know what their reports are working on, wonders if they're micromanaging, or has managers who aren't managing their own people. Grounded in [Lencioni — *The Motive*](../../references/lencioni_the-motive.md) (Omission 2), [Grove — *High Output Management*](../../references/grove_high-output-management.md) (TRM), and [Sanchez — *Main Street Millionaire*](../../references/sanchez_main-street-millionaire.md).

**Scope seam:** this flow builds the *ongoing, per-person* management system. If the user needs check-ins for one specific task they've handed off, that's the delegation flow's job — `../delegation/phases/5-monitoring.md` sets a per-task cadence calibrated to TRM. Run this flow when the gap is the relationship, not the task.

---

## When to run this flow

- "I don't actually know what my reports are doing day to day"
- "Am I micromanaging if I ask?" / "I trust them, so I stay out of it"
- "My managers don't manage their people"
- Small problems keep surfacing late, as big ones
- The motive gate flagged "managing my people" as something the user was trying to avoid

---

## The frame (grounded in *The Motive* — and the skill's second spine line)

The definition is the load-bearing line of the whole skill:

> **"Management is the act of aligning people's actions, behaviors, and attitudes with the needs of the organization and making sure that little problems don't become big ones."** — Lencioni, *The Motive*

Everything in this flow serves that sentence. Lencioni is explicit about what management is *not*: it's not writing detailed objectives and running performance reviews (*"This is not really management"*), and it's not a sign of distrust. His two reframes are the heart of the coaching:

- *"Of course, trusting someone is not an excuse for not managing them."* Trust is not a management strategy.
- *"Helping subordinates establish a direction and knowing how they are progressing is far from micromanagement."* Knowing what your people are doing is the job, not overreach.

Managing individuals is three things: (1) helping them set the **general direction** of their work, (2) ensuring it's **aligned with and understood by their peers** and staying **informed enough to catch obstacles early**, and (3) **coaching them behaviorally** to succeed. Grove's Task-Relevant Maturity calibrates *how much* monitoring each person needs on each task — low TRM gets more structure, high TRM gets more space, and TRM is task-specific, not a global trait.

And the piece senior leaders skip most: **the cascade.** *"They must ensure that their subordinates one level below are managing their people too."* Even good people-managers forget to demand it of their own managers. Sanchez's line for the leader still doing the work themselves: *"don't get a dog and then do the barking."*

## The flow (run the moves in order)

### Move 1 — Surface what the user actually knows (Listen)

> **Question:** Pick one report you've been light on. Without checking anything — what are the two or three things they're working on right now, and how's each going?

The gaps in the answer are the diagnosis. If the user can't answer, that's not trust, that's absence. Name it gently.

### Move 2 — Run the reframe (Educate + Apply)

Whichever dodge shows up, meet it with the matching reframe:

- "I don't want to micromanage" → knowing direction and progress is *not* micromanagement. Micromanaging is dictating *how*; managing is aligning *what* and catching problems early.
- "I trust them" → trust isn't the question. You can fully trust someone and still owe them alignment and early problem-catching. Trust is not a substitute for management.
- "They're senior, they shouldn't need it" → management doesn't scale down with seniority. Senior people have higher-stakes small-problems-becoming-big ones.

Calibrate with TRM: for this person on this work, are they high maturity (needs light touch, more autonomy) or still building it (needs more structure)? Match the cadence to that, not to their title.

**The self-check (ask these verbatim — a "yes" is the tell).** Lencioni closes this omission with three reflection questions. Ask them straight, and hold the user to the answer:

> **Ask yourself:**
> - *"Do you believe that providing individual guidance and coaching to your people is somehow beneath you or not worth your time?"*
> - *"Do you feel that you should be able to trust them to manage themselves?"*
> - *"Do you justify not knowing what your direct reports are doing by claiming not to want to be a micromanager?"*

A "yes" to any of these means, in Lencioni's words, *"your motive for leading may be off."* Call it out — the second and third questions are the exact dodges from Move 2 in question form. Name the "yes," then build the cadence that answers it.

### Move 3 — Build the cadence (Apply + Execute)

Design a repeatable structure that delivers the three functions. Land specifics:

- **Direction:** how the user and report agree on the general direction of the work (not task lists — direction and priorities).
- **Progress visibility:** the recurring touchpoint (1:1 cadence, written update, whatever fits) where the user stays informed enough to catch obstacles early. Set an actual frequency.
- **Behavioral coaching:** the one behavior this person needs to develop to succeed, and how the user will coach it. (If it's a hard behavior to raise, route to `../difficult-conversations/flow.md`.)

### Move 4 — The cascade check (Execute)

Ask directly:

> **Question:** Are the managers who report to you doing this same thing for their people — and how do you know?

If the user can't say, the plan gains a fourth line: how they'll check and reinforce that their managers manage. Even a great people-manager who skips this leaves the org unmanaged one level down.

## Pre-empted shortcuts

- **Don't let "I trust them" end the conversation.** Trust is not management. The reframe *is* the coaching — deliver it.
- **Don't build a performance-review process.** Lencioni is explicit that objectives-and-reviews is not management. The cadence is about direction, live progress, and behavior — not paperwork.
- **Don't skip the cascade.** A cadence that stops at the user's own reports leaves everyone below them unmanaged. Always run Move 4.
- **Don't set a cadence with no frequency.** "We'll check in regularly" is not output. Name the actual rhythm.
